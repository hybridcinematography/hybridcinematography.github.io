Abstract

On a film set, the camera move is committed during a take. Generative video reshooting lets filmmakers change it afterward, but may require hallucinating unrecorded content, a gap sometimes discovered only after leaving the set. We present Hybrid Cinematography, a workflow that bridges physical capture and generative reshooting to manage hallucination risk while filmmakers can still act on it. Using an editable 3D shot plan and a proxy of the take, our previsualization evaluates hallucination risk in real time. Seeing where the take lacks support, filmmakers can iteratively adjust the plan, explore moves that balance capture and generation, shoot guided pickups, or knowingly accept hallucination. We demonstrate the workflow through a mobile augmented reality application for on-set planning, capture, and review, and an offline pipeline for existing video. A study with experienced filmmakers reveals how previsualizing risk informs camera decisions and exposes tensions between creative intent and generative hallucination.

How far you can move depends on what you captured

A new camera move can reveal surfaces, details, or views of a performance that the recorded take never captured. The model must invent that missing content, potentially changing the scene or the performer. These changes may only become apparent after generation, when the chance to capture more has passed.

An on-set example

Planning a shot the phone cannot capture

Some cinematic shots are difficult to execute by hand. Here, we plan a dolly zoom followed by a push through a hole in a Connect Four board. The phone cannot fit through the opening, so we plan this move as a generative reshoot.

Plan, capture, and review

After planning the shot, we capture an offset pass over the board. We then scrub between the captured and target camera moves to inspect hallucination risk and record guided pickups where coverage is missing.

Live capture · LiDAR. The app builds its 3D proxy in real time from LiDAR depth and tracked camera poses, so the take can be reviewed on set.

Author the camera in space and time

Space · Where the camera goes
Place keyframe A at the start, dolly zoom into B, set C just before the hole, and push through to D. These AR keyframes specify the camera's position, viewing direction, and framing.
Time · When it gets there
Set the total duration, then adjust speed and easing along each segment to speed up or slow down the camera. The recorded performance keeps its original timing.

Expressing the exact path, framing, and changing pace in a text prompt can be cumbersome. The shot plan specifies them directly. Rendering the proxy along that plan produces a point-cloud conditioning video that encodes both space and time for Vista4D, Gen3C, or Gemini, alongside any source footage and masks required by the model.

Each clip retains its own timing. The two outputs are separate model runs; play them individually or expand a video to inspect the result.
